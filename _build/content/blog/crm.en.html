CRM: everything about the customer

A CRM (Customer Relationship Management) system manages sales and customer relationships. It holds the customer base, the sales stages, tasks, the full history of communication with each customer and the results of each sales manager.

You need a CRM if: enquiries get lost, managers keep customer history in Excel and messaging apps, and the sales plan is hard to track.

ERP: the company's internal resources

An ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning) system brings a company's internal operations together: HR, finance, inventory, purchasing and sales. The goal is for every department to work with the same data.

You need an ERP if: stock levels do not match the accounts, reports take days to compile, and departments pass data to each other by hand.

Ready-made platform or from scratch?

Ready-made platforms such as ERPNext already cover many standard processes and can be put into operation quickly. If your processes are close to the standard, this is the fastest and most cost-effective route: only the places that need it are customised.

If your business model is non-standard and a ready-made system would constantly have to be "bent" out of shape, a system built from scratch is more convenient and cheaper in the long run.

The worst option is breaking processes that work in order to fit them to a system. The system should serve the process, not the other way round.

What if you need both?

In growing companies CRM and ERP usually work together: a deal closed in the CRM automatically becomes an order, a stock movement and an invoice in the ERP. All it takes is a properly built integration between the two systems.

Implementation stages

  1. Audit. We write down the current processes, the spreadsheets in use and the problems.
  2. Pilot. We launch the system in one department first, such as sales, and test it on real work.
  3. Data migration. The customer base, products and stock balances are cleaned and moved into the new system.
  4. Training. Short, practical training is held for each role.
  5. Expansion. After the pilot, the other departments are connected in stages.

The most common mistakes

  • Migrating "dirty" data. Duplicates and outdated records move into the new system too and reduce trust in it.
  • No owner for the system. There has to be one person inside the company who makes decisions and is responsible for the process.
  • Customising everything. Where a standard feature is enough, extra code only makes support more expensive.
  • Saving on training. If employees do not understand the system, they go back to Excel.

In short

  • CRM is for customers and sales; ERP is for internal resources.
  • If the problem is with customers, start with CRM; if it is internal disorder, start with ERP.
  • For standard processes, a ready-made platform; for non-standard ones, a custom system.
  • The two deliver the greatest effect together, connected by integration.

Does a small company need an ERP?

Not yet, if operations are comfortably managed with a few spreadsheets: there is no need to rush. The time for an ERP has come when data starts to mismatch between departments and reports start to arrive late.

Can you use a CRM without an ERP?

Yes. Many companies start with a CRM and add an ERP as their internal processes become more complex.
