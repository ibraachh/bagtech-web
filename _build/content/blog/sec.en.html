Accounts and access

  1. Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA). Especially for email, accounting and admin panels. Even if a password is stolen, the second step protects the account.
  2. Use strong passwords and never reuse them. A password manager makes it easy to have a separate password for every service.
  3. Grant only as much access as is needed. Each employee should have access only to the systems their own work requires.
  4. Close the accounts of employees who leave, immediately. This is the step that is forgotten most often.

Systems and data

  1. Install updates on time. Automatic updates for operating systems, browsers and server software are the simplest defence.
  2. Follow the 3-2-1 backup rule. Keep 3 copies of your data on 2 different media, with 1 of them outside the office. Test the restore from time to time.
  3. Encrypt your data. Disk encryption on laptops and HTTPS on your website are the minimum.

People and processes

  1. Teach employees to recognise phishing. Fake emails are still the main starting point of attacks.
  2. Log and review access. Who signed in, when and from where: this information is invaluable during an incident.
  3. Prepare an incident plan. Write down in advance who will be called, which systems will be isolated and how recovery will work when something happens.
Security is not a one-off project, it is a habit. Review this list again every quarter.

How to recognise a phishing email

  • The sender's address resembles a familiar name, but the domain is different: for example, one letter has been changed.
  • The email demands urgent action: "your account will be closed", "payment must be made today".
  • When you hover over a link, the address shown does not match the text in the email.
  • An unexpected attachment arrives, especially an archive or a document with macros.

The rule is simple: if in doubt, do not click the link; contact the sender through another channel, such as a phone call.

Why you need to test your backups

Ransomware is malicious software that encrypts and locks files and demands money to unlock them. In that situation the only reliable way out is restoring from a backup. Yet many companies find out at exactly that moment that their backup does not work. So run a real restore test at least once a quarter, and make sure the off-site copy is kept separate from your main network.

Where to start?

If you cannot do everything at once, start with 2FA, backups and updates: these three steps significantly reduce the risk from the most common types of attack. After that, an audit of your systems will show the weak spots and the priorities.

In short

  • The first three priorities: 2FA, backups and updates.
  • Keep access minimal and close it on time.
  • Employees are the most important line of defence.
  • The incident plan must be written before the incident.

What is two-factor authentication (2FA)?

Two-factor authentication (2FA) is a second confirmation step in addition to the password when signing in to an account, such as a code from an app on your phone. Even if the password is stolen, the second step protects the account.

What is the 3-2-1 backup rule?

The 3-2-1 backup rule means keeping 3 copies of your data on 2 different media, with 1 of them outside the office.

What is phishing?

Phishing is a fake email or message that appears to come from a trusted sender; its purpose is to capture passwords or payment details.
